# Meta-review: Submission 2417

**Meta-review summary**

The paper asks whether a LoRA-adapted Qwen2-VL-2B grounder benefits from being told the action type, comparing four conditioning mechanisms (auxiliary loss B, hard routing C, additive embedding D-hook, prepended token D-token) against a flat baseline under matched data, compute and decoding, with a taps-and-swipes control, a 300-5,000 scaling study, gold/wrong/zero interventions, and a documented `inputs_embeds`/M-RoPE false negative. The design is unusually careful for a workshop submission and the reporting is honest: the authors' own hypothesis is refuted, a retracted result is disclosed, per-class and per-probe numbers are given, and all four reviewers credit this. The evidence supports a narrower claim than the abstract states. B and D-hook beat A only on a mix whose sole "spatial informativeness" is a 14% class of ungroundable type events with a sentinel target; the gain is entirely a click gain (Table 6); nothing helps on clicks plus scrolls; and the paper's own qualitative description (conditioning "rescues clicks that the baseline sends off-screen", l.214-216, Fig. 4) is exactly what the reviewers' alternative reading predicts, namely that type supervision lets the grounder partition off examples whose targets are not on the screen. The reviewers agree on this confound (R1, R3, R4), on the statistics (all four: the 1,250 pooled (seed, example) units are 250 examples, and the reported CIs are narrower than the seed-level uncertainty of the baseline alone), on the underpowered single-seed D-hook zeroing claim that appears in the abstract (R2, R3), and on numbers that do not reconcile across tables (R1, R2, R4). They split on the decision (R3 poster; R1, R2, R4 reject) but not on substance: R2 and R4 say explicitly that re-analysis without new compute would flip them, and R1 calls it "close; fixable". I verified the two prior-work claims and the HF code behavior (above). My own reading is that the experimental design is sound, the inference and framing are overstated, every consequential fix is re-analysis, rewriting, or cheap re-evaluation of existing checkpoints, and the corrected paper is useful to this audience.

**Consequential issues**

1. **The headline attribution is confounded by the ungroundable type class.** Raised by R1 (W1), R3 (W1), R4 (W2). I agree. The only difference between `all_with_coords` and the control is 35/250 type events with a sentinel target on which every variant scores exactly 0. Flat A scores 0.256 on clicks in the headline mix but 0.382 overall on the click/scroll control (different slices, so indicative only), and the conditioned variants' click rates (0.32-0.36) approach that control figure. "Action type predicts the target location" (abstract, Sec. 5.2 title, conclusion) and "action type identifies examples whose target is not on the screen" fit the existing data equally well; the Limitations concede this (l.274-275) but the abstract, title and conclusion do not. *Minimum fix (re-analysis + rewriting):* state how the sentinel is serialized during training and report, from the logged predictions, what fraction of A's click misses are sentinel or off-screen emissions versus on-screen-but-wrong, for A and each conditioned variant; then restate the claim as "type supervision protects click grounding from ungroundable examples in a mixed-action training stream" unless that analysis shows otherwise. *Stronger fix (new but cheap experiment, roughly 15 L4 runs):* R3's degenerate-class control (add a synthetic sentinel class to `taps_and_swipes`) or R1's clicks+scrolls training at n=1,200 scored on the same 215 click/scroll validation examples. Either outcome is publishable; only the framing changes.

2. **The inference unit is wrong and the stars are unearned as stated.** Raised by all four (R1 W5, R2 W1, R3 W6, R4 W1). I agree. The abstract's "paired bootstrap tests over 1,250 examples" is 250 examples times five seeds; App. A acknowledges same-example correlation but not same-seed correlation, which is the larger one. A's seed-level SE alone is 0.043/sqrt(5) = 0.019, larger than the bootstrap SE of about 0.011 implied by the +/-0.022 half-widths; a five-seed sign test cannot go below p = 0.0625, so no *** is possible at the seed level; the permutation test shares the assumption; validation steps are also episode-clustered. R2's unpaired seed-level tests (B-A t about 3.0, D-hook-A t about 2.0) are conservative because all variants share the per-seed data order, so I expect B > A and probably D-hook > A to survive at lower confidence, while "C gains", "D-token worse than B", and "C harms" on the control may not. *Minimum fix (re-analysis only, no compute):* a cluster bootstrap over examples keeping all seeds plus a seed-level paired test with the five per-seed differences shown for every contrast in Tables 1-4 and 7; delete "1,250 examples"; keep only stars that survive; replace "tied", "change nothing", "within noise" with CI bounds (R2 W4: the control CIs [-0.038, +0.012] and [-0.047, +0.010] admit harms nearly as large as the headline gain).

3. **The abstract-level mechanism claim about D-hook rests on one seed and 120 examples, with a non-comparable zero condition, and is contradicted by the wrong-map result.** Raised by R2 (W2), R3 (W2, W3), R1 (W7). I agree. "Can be zeroed at test time without loss" is Table 4's -0.033 [-0.117, +0.042]; the interval allows zeroing to remove about 80% of the +0.054 headline gain, while the opposing D-token test got five seeds times 250 examples. Zero is D-hook's initialization and equals A's input, but a zero-norm slot is out of distribution for D-token, so the training-time/inference-time dichotomy is partly by construction. D-hook's per-class embedding norms are never reported; if E[click] is near zero (68% of examples), zero-insensitivity is nearly a no-op. Meanwhile a wrong type collapses D-hook from 0.358 to 0.08-0.10, more than D-token, so the conclusion's "an additive embedding that the model can ignore at test time" and the practitioner lesson "prefer conditioning the model does not depend on at inference" do not follow: D-hook is robust to removal, not to corruption. *Minimum fix (cheap re-evaluation of existing checkpoints + rewriting, no training):* D-hook interventions on all five seeds times 250 examples with an A row, per-class norms of E, per-class (click/scroll) results, and a class-mean uninformative condition for both mechanisms; rewrite Sec. 6.3, the abstract and the conclusion accordingly.

4. **Numbers do not reconcile across the paper.** Raised by R1 (W6), R2 (W5), R4 (W1). I agree and checked: Table 6 implies C hit@0.10 = 0.272 while Table 1 says 0.264 (the other four rows reconcile within rounding); Table 3 "gold" differs from Table 1's D-token row in every column for the same models on the same set; Sec. 6.1's A = 0.390 and D-hook = 0.392 "on the same control" versus Table 2's 0.382 and 0.363 (a tie becomes -0.018); Sec. 5.4's "about a third" is 0.021/0.037 = 57%, and Table 7 implies three-seed D-hook = 0.300, not 0.292; 18+11+45+90 = 164 of 169 clicks; "ten points" versus "nine-point". None of these systematically favor the paper, so I read them as different runs reported without saying so, or non-deterministic evaluation, rather than anything worse, but they undermine trust in a paper whose value is its care. *Fix (re-analysis/rewriting):* state which runs each table and sentence come from, re-derive every quoted number from the final logs, and confirm greedy evaluation is deterministic.

5. **The closest prior work is missing and it preempts the framing.** Raised by R1 (W3), R4 (W3); I verified both. CoCo-Agent (ACL Findings 2024) decomposes AITW action prediction into "action type prediction and action target conditioned on the action type" in one stream, which is variant C. LiMAC (ICLR 2025) puts a small action-type transformer ahead of a LoRA-tuned Qwen2-VL-2B (or Florence-2) on AitW and AndroidControl, selecting click targets conditioned on the predicted type (via UI elements rather than pixels) and invoking the VLM only for text actions. The title, abstract and contribution (1) sell the what/where factorization; what is new here is the matched-compute mechanism comparison, the interventions, and the negative result on the prepended token. *Fix (rewriting):* cite and position against both, reframe contribution (1) as a mechanism study, and correct the GUI-Libra characterization (its action-aware supervision is token reweighting, not a type loss).

6. **The scaling study is confounded and over-read.** Raised by R2 (W3), R4 (W2). I agree. Each n is scored on a different validation slice of unreported class mix (A swings 0.139-0.363 non-monotonically), which matters because the gain is a click gain; Table 7 has 24 uncorrected cells; "hurts at one" (abstract) is one cell; "differ everywhere else" (l.212) infers a B-versus-D-hook difference from one contrast being starred and the other not; at n=300 B-A is +0.067*** on hit@0.10 and -0.055*** on hit@0.25. *Fix (re-analysis + rewriting):* present the study as descriptive, test B against D-hook directly wherever a difference is asserted, and report each slice's class mix. If checkpoints exist, re-score every model on the common never-trained-on slice (steps 5,000-5,249); if they do not, this is not required for acceptance.

**Minor issues**

- The M-RoPE explanation is plausible but unverified and probably wrong in detail (R3 W5). In HF v4.46.0, `get_rope_index` scans `input_ids` for vision tokens, and when `position_ids` reach the text model as `None` they become sequential 1-D positions replicated across all three axes for every token, so image tokens lose their (h, w) layout entirely rather than being "positioned as if the slot were not there"; the visual `masked_scatter` also runs only on the `input_ids` path, so hand-built `inputs_embeds` had to scatter features itself, a second candidate cause. State the transformers version, cite issue #35463 / PR #35466, and either inspect `position_ids` under D-slot or hedge the mechanism; demote the pitfall from an abstract-level contribution to a cited, version-stamped section (R4 W4), but keep it: the silent-failure-with-images case is not what the issue documents and is useful to this audience.
- Attention analysis (R3 W4): teacher-forcing means the y-predicting token already has the gold x in its prefix, so "localization happens once x has been emitted" is partly the artifact; one layer, head-averaged, no flat-A row, not conditioned on correctness. The gold/wrong/zero contrasts remain interpretable since they share the confound; the absolute "four times chance" and the "evidence localization" framing in Sec. 2 should be softened.
- Report parse-failure rates per variant and C's numbers excluding failures (R2 W7); C's mean-distance loss may be a formatting artifact.
- The D-token refutation is conditional on one learning rate and a fresh N(0, 0.02^2) token (R1 W4, R4 W5, R2 W7); the abstract should carry the caveat the Limitations already state. A text-prompt variant ("Action: click", zero new parameters) is the missing obvious baseline and is cheap; a constant-slot D-token control would separate conditioning gain from foreign-token cost. Recommended, not required.
- End to end (Sec. 5.4): no CI for predicted-versus-flat (+0.016, three seeds); clarify whether the Stage-1 MLP was checkpoint-selected on the same validation examples used for evaluation (R2 Q6, R4 Q4); note that D-hook is the variant the paper says does not need the signal.
- The Mind2Web control is at floor (10% in-element), so it is uninformative in either direction; stop citing it as confirming the factorization (l.189-194; R4 W2, and this weakens R1's use of it too).
- Seeds 45-46 were added after seeing three-seed results and lowered A from 0.255 to 0.229, raising the effects (R2 W7, R4 W1); the disclosure is good, but state the rationale.
- Data protocol: name the AITW subset, report how many episodes the validation slice spans, and note that sequential slicing can share the boundary episode with training (R1 W8, R2 W1).
- Contribution (1) claims four mechanisms on two datasets; only D-hook ran on Mind2Web and there is no table (R1 W6).
- Presentation: Fig. 2 is unreadable at column width, Fig. 4 screenshots are illegible, Sec. C is an empty heading, Fig. 5 duplicates Tables 1-2, and no code/split release is promised (R4 W7).
- The introduction's compounding-error motivation is untested (R4 W6, R2 W6); tone it down rather than add long-horizon evaluation.

**Reviewer points I discount**

- R1 (W2) and partly R4 (W6): the demand for a grounding-pretrained backbone, leaderboard comparability and AITW action-matching numbers as a condition of acceptance. This is a conference-level bar; the workshop explicitly invites small-scale studies, the matched-compute comparison is internally valid, and the paper says plainly that absolute numbers are far below the field. The one-learning-rate caveat should reach the abstract, but this is not a rejection ground.
- R1 (W2): "the baseline's seed variance is 3-10x that of B/D-hook". A is 0.043, B 0.021 (2x), D-hook 0.041 (about 1x), C 0.045, D-token 0.049. Only B is markedly more stable; the "under-tuned baseline stabilized by the signal" reading does not extend to D-hook.
- R1 (W1): "a deployed pipeline never grounds type events, so the practical lesson is nil". That depends on the training regime: native agent models train on mixed action streams, so type supervision during adaptation on mixed data is a relevant lesson, just not the one the abstract currently states.
- R3 (W2): reading D-token's near-identical Table 4 values under all perturbations (0.117/0.142/0.142) as evidence of OOD brittleness rather than use. The five-seed, 250-example Table 3 clearly separates wrong (0.079) from zero (0.167), so the single-seed equality is noise. The underlying point, that zero is not the same operation for the two architectures and a class-mean condition is needed, stands and is in issue 3.
- R4 (W4): "contribution (4) is a documented library behavior". Issue #35463 reports a crash when generating from `inputs_embeds` and the PR fixes the no-image case; the silent mispositioning with images present that the paper describes is not on record there. Demote and cite; do not delete.
- R2 (W1): the specific seed-level p-values (B-A p about 0.03, D-hook-A p about 0.08) come from unpaired tests on reported stds, which are conservative because all variants share the per-seed data order. The direction of the critique is right; the numbers are lower bounds on the evidence, which is why I expect the headline contrasts to survive.
- R4 (W2): the observation that the official AITW metric matches swipes by axis only, so hit@0.10 on a swipe touch point is questionable. Fair, but D-hook improves scrolls in Table 6, so the touch points are not arbitrary, and the paper's control does not hinge on it.

**Decision**: Accept (poster).

The workshop's bar is "sound, honest, useful to the audience", not conference-level novelty, and the 2025 edition accepted small-scale and negative-result papers. This paper's experimental design (matched compute, seeds, a control mix, interventions, a disclosed retraction) is sounder than most of what the workshop sees, its honesty is exemplary, and its content after correction is useful on three of the workshop's stated topics: training paradigms for grounded VLMs (type supervision during adaptation on mixed action streams, with a mechanism ranking), interpretability (the gold/wrong/zero intervention template), and failure analysis (a silent positional-encoding failure that inverted a conclusion). What is wrong is the inference and the framing, not the experiments: the stars are computed on the wrong unit, the abstract's mechanistic sentence rests on one seed, the headline is attributed to a spatial prior when the data equally support a label-partitioning effect, tables disagree, and two directly relevant prior papers are missing. Every one of those fixes is re-analysis, rewriting, or re-evaluating checkpoints that already exist; the one new experiment that would settle the framing costs roughly fifteen 35-minute runs. Three reviewers recommend rejection, but two of them state that re-analysis alone would flip them and the third calls it "close; fixable"; the underlying B > A and D-hook > A contrasts very likely survive even conservative seed-level tests. The corrected paper is a clean mixed-result mechanism study that this audience should see. Not a spotlight: the headline is neither crisp nor, as currently stated, earned, and the camera-ready must carry the reframing and corrected statistics below.

**If the authors could make exactly five changes before the camera-ready or a resubmission, they should be (in priority order):**

1. Reframe the headline around the type-class confound: report how the sentinel is serialized and what fraction of the flat baseline's click misses are sentinel/off-screen emissions, rewrite the abstract, Sec. 5.2 title and conclusion as "type supervision protects click grounding from ungroundable examples in a mixed-action stream" unless a degenerate-class control or a clicks+scrolls run at n=1,200 on the same validation examples shows the gain survives without the type class.
2. Redo every contrast with a cluster bootstrap over the 250 examples (keeping all seeds) plus a seed-level paired test showing the five per-seed differences, delete "1,250 examples", keep only the stars that survive, replace "tied/nothing/within noise" with CI bounds, and present the scaling study as descriptive with a direct B-versus-D-hook test wherever a difference is asserted.
3. Re-run the D-hook interventions on all five seeds times 250 examples with an A row, report per-class norms of E and per-class click/scroll results, add a class-mean uninformative condition for both mechanisms, and rewrite the mechanism claim as "robust to removal, not to corruption", removing "can be zeroed without loss" and "prefer conditioning the model does not depend on at inference" unless the full-power numbers support them.
4. Reconcile every number (Sec. 6.1 vs Table 2; Table 1 vs Table 3; Table 6 vs Table 1 for C; the Sec. 5.4 arithmetic and 0.292 vs 0.300; 164 vs 169 clicks; the empty Sec. C), state which runs each table comes from, and state the transformers version.
5. Cite and position against CoCo-Agent (conditional action prediction is variant C) and LiMAC (a type-first pipeline with LoRA-tuned Qwen2-VL-2B on the same data), reframe contribution (1) as a mechanism comparison, correct the GUI-Libra description, and demote the M-RoPE pitfall to a cited, version-stamped section with its mechanism either verified against `position_ids` or corrected to the 1-D fallback.
