**Meta-review summary**

The paper asks what supervising the action type does for coordinate grounding when Qwen2-VL-2B is LoRA-tuned on Android in the Wild, comparing a flat baseline with five matched-compute conditioning mechanisms (auxiliary loss B, hard routing C, additive embedding D-hook, prepended learned token D-token, type-as-prompt-word D-text) over five seeds with a cluster bootstrap and seed-level paired tests. On a stream whose type events carry a sentinel target clamped to (0,0), B, D-hook and D-text gain 5–7 hit@0.10 points and D-token does not; removing the sentinel events lifts the flat baseline to the conditioned models' level, a taps-and-swipes control shows no gain, wrong/zero/class-mean interventions show both learned embeddings are read at inference, and an `inputs_embeds`/M-RoPE pitfall that produced a false nine-point negative is documented. The evidence supports the mechanism ranking on that stream, the clean-stream null, the "consulted at inference" reading of the interventions, and the library diagnosis (I verified in the current transformers source that without `input_ids` positions collapse to a 1-D index replicated over the three axes, and that HF issue #35463 is the December 2024 crash report the paper describes). It does not support the abstract's framing: the sentinel class is the authors' own serialization applied to a stream from which the type has been stripped; the D-token "refutation" tests an embedding that, by arithmetic the reviewers give and I confirmed (0.02·√1536 = 0.78, the reported trained norm, while the D-hook table trained from zero under the same optimizer reached only 0.04), barely left its random initialization; the "returns to baseline when zeroed" claim rests on an interval that admits the opposite; and the deployment-relevant end-to-end margin is null and omitted from the abstract. All four reviewers agree on these points and on the paper's unusual candor and careful design; they disagree only on the decision, with R3 accepting conditionally and R1/R2/R4 rejecting "in the current framing" while each states that a reframed version would be a credible poster. Having checked the disputed technical claims, I find the reviewers right on substance, but nearly every fix is rewriting or re-analysis plus a few cheap runs, which puts the revised paper above this workshop's bar.

**Consequential issues**

1. **The headline positive result depends on a preprocessing choice, and the abstract/title lead with it.** Raised by R1 (W1), R4 (W1), R2 (Limitations), R3 (W6). I agree in substance. The "degenerate class" exists because the serializer clamps AITW's (−1,−1) sentinel to (0,0) and supervises the coordinate loss on it in a stream where the model is not told the type; native formats either emit no coordinate for type actions (UI-TARS/OS-Atlas style) or emit the type before the sentinel (Auto-UI style), so baseline A does not stand for any deployed design. The paper's own Table 7 (A rises 0.255→0.297 when the class is removed; B and D-hook then add +0.021 [−0.004, +0.045] and +0.008 [−0.020, +0.035]) and Table 3 already establish this, and Section 5.1 draws the right conclusion ("protection... we do not claim a general spatial prior"). What I do not accept is the reviewers' stronger reading that this makes the study uninformative: the observation that a sentinel class degrades click grounding well beyond exact (0,0) emissions, the matched ranking of mechanisms, and the clean-stream null are real, modest contributions. Minimum fix: **rewriting** of title, abstract, the Section 5.1 heading and the conclusion so that the clean-stream null and the mechanism ranking are the headline and the sentinel-class protection is a diagnosis of a preprocessing choice. No new experiments are required (Table 7 already is the artifact-free stream); extending Table 7 to D-text and five seeds is a cheap improvement.

2. **The D-token refutation is confounded by an effectively untrained embedding.** Raised by R1 (W6), R2 (W3), R3 (W1), R4 (W2). I agree and verified the arithmetic: N(0, 0.02²) in d=1536 has expected norm 0.784; the paper reports trained rows at 0.78; and under the identical AdamW schedule the zero-initialized D-hook table moved only to norm 0.04, so the D-token rows moved by roughly 5% of their norm. What was tested is a fixed, full-norm random class code plus LoRA; what works best (D-text, +0.073) is a prepended pretrained embedding, so "the prepended-token mechanism" is not refuted, only this configuration. The intervention results for D-token remain valid (the model demonstrably reads the code). Section 7 concedes the untuned schedule but the abstract states the refutation as a finding. Minimum fix: **rewriting** (narrow the claim to the tested configuration) plus **re-analysis** (report ‖E_trained − E_init‖ and pairwise cosines if checkpoints exist). Proper fix: **new experiment, cheap** — D-token with a separate 10–100× embedding learning rate or word-embedding initialization, and the frozen-slot control on the fixed path; five seeds at ~35 min on an L4 is about 3 GPU-hours.

3. **The bootstrap clusters on steps, not episodes.** Raised by all four (R1 W3, R2 W1, R3 W3, R4 W5). I agree. Validation slices are 250 consecutive steps from "a few dozen episodes"; steps in an episode share app, goal and near-identical screens, so the step-level cluster bootstrap is anti-conservative, and a boundary episode may be shared with training. The seed-level test is immune to this correlation (but has n=5) and the paper's "established only when both agree" rule offers real protection: B's headline (+0.064 [+0.036, +0.093], seed p=0.004) survives a design effect of 3, whereas D-hook (+0.054 [+0.022, +0.086]) and D-text (+0.073 [+0.033, +0.113]) become marginal, and many secondary stars (Table 4 class-mean, Table 7 B-click, most of Table 8) may not survive. Minimum fix: **re-analysis only** — episode ids are in the data; recompute every interval with episodes as clusters, report episodes per slice, drop the shared boundary episode, and state which stars survive.

4. **Null results are asserted as equalities, and the deployment claim rests on the less realistic perturbation.** Raised by R2 (W2), R3 (W2), R4 (W3), R1 (W5). I agree. "With its embedding zeroed, the additive model returns to the baseline's accuracy" rests on gold−zero = +0.027 [−0.006, +0.060] (seed p=0.22), an interval that admits both "costs nothing" and "removes most of the +0.046 gain on these examples"; Appendix G reads the same quantity as "a small real cost". The end-to-end predicted-type margin (+0.016 [−0.013, +0.047], seed p=0.44) is the actual deployment number and is absent from the abstract, and under a wrong type, the realistic failure at 84% Stage-1 accuracy (against a 67.6% majority class), D-hook (0.054 / 0.099) is more brittle than D-token (0.079 / 0.155), the opposite of the abstract's ordering. "No mechanism beats it" after type removal ignores B's established click (+0.039 [+0.004, +0.075]) and hit@0.25 (+0.053 [+0.024, +0.083]) gains. Minimum fix: **rewriting** — report intervals, use an equivalence framing with a stated margin wherever an equality is claimed, put the end-to-end null and the wrong-type collapse in the abstract, and keep the one robust contrast (zeroed D-token −0.067 [−0.098, −0.038] vs. A; zeroed D-hook +0.019, n.s.) stated as such. Optional **new experiment, inference only**: confidence-gated zeroing end-to-end, which would turn "degrades to baseline" into a measured benefit (R3).

5. **D-text, the best and parameter-free mechanism, is absent from every secondary analysis.** Raised by all four. I agree. It has no per-class row (Table 2), no control (Table 3), no type-removed run (Table 7), no intervention, no scaling and no end-to-end run, and the abstract's "6 to 10 points" on clicks is unsupported for it (Table 1 overall deltas are mixed with Table 2 per-class ones). Minimum fix: **re-analysis** for the Table 2 row (per-example logs exist) and **new experiments, cheap** for Tables 3 and 7 at three seeds, a wrong-word intervention (inference only), and end-to-end with predicted types.

6. **The scaling study scores each size on a different slice with a different class mix, yet makes a cross-size claim.** Raised by R1 (W3), R2 (W5), R4 (W5). I agree with respect to the one claim "D-hook's advantage persists with data and B's does not" (l. 241): scroll counts range from 12 to 71 across sizes, B hurts scrolls and D-hook helps them (Table 2), so the two negative B−A cells (n=800, 71 scrolls; n=2500, 53 scrolls) are at least partly composition. Within-size contrasts are fine and the paper says so. Minimum fix: **rewriting** (drop the persist/not-persist contrast) or **re-analysis** (per-class curves from logs); best fix: re-evaluate saved checkpoints on one held-out slice beyond step 5,000 (inference only if checkpoints were kept, otherwise retraining).

**Minor issues**

- D-hook is described as adding E[a] to "every text and image token embedding" (l. 134–135). I confirmed in the current source that `get_input_embeddings()(input_ids)` runs first and image features are `masked_scatter`ed over the placeholders afterwards, so a hook on the embedding module cannot reach image positions (R3 W7, R4 W6). Results are unaffected; state where the hook sits and correct the sentence.
- The M-RoPE diagnosis matches the library (verified) but was inferred rather than confirmed in the authors' own runs, and the before/after compares D-slot on the buggy path with D-hook on the fixed path (R3 W4); a flat-A run through `inputs_embeds` with and without explicit 3-D position ids would settle it cheaply, otherwise soften "corrected account".
- The "uninformative" controls are not matched across architectures (R3 W5): the mean of three near-orthogonal 0.78-norm rows has norm ≈0.45, so it is not "in-distribution norm" for D-token; add a fresh N(0, 0.02²) draw as the D-token control (inference only).
- Hard routing's promised "both ways" numbers are never tabulated (R2 W4); C's Table 3 mean-L2 increase (+0.086) is about what 6–7% unparseable outputs at √2 would produce. Tabulate parsed-only numbers and per-class parse rates from the logs.
- hit@0.10 is non-standard for AITW; I confirmed the official matcher uses `_TAP_DISTANCE_THRESHOLD = 0.14` plus augmented bbox containment (R2 W7). Add hit@0.14 from the logs.
- "Protection from the degenerate class" is never characterized beyond exact (0,0) emissions (R3 W6, R2 Q7); an origin-bias analysis of A's click errors from the per-example predictions would substantiate "degrades more broadly".
- State whether B's pooled z̄ includes teacher-forced answer tokens, which reveal the type through "(0, 0)" (R3 Q5).
- Non-standard split (stored-order slices rather than official episode splits) and no action history although AITW goals are episode-level (R1 W2): state as limitations; they limit comparability, not the matched comparison.
- Wording: "the only difference between this stream and the control is the type class" (n_train, n_val, seeds and slice differ); "distance is exactly √2 whatever the model predicts" (only with a capped penalty); "an earlier version of this paper" reads as revision notes; Table 9's caption still says "pooled examples".
- Related work: the LiMAC sentence is ambiguous about which module selects click targets (I checked: AcT selects them contrastively over UI-element embeddings and the VLM only generates text for input-text/open-app); add a clause. Narrow "what has not been measured" to acknowledge CoCo-Agent's own CAP ablation (R4), and consider TinyClick, AutoGLM and the I-MRoPE/position-to-coordinate work (R1, R4).
- Presentation: Appendices C, D and H are headings whose tables/figures floated elsewhere; Figures 1 and 4 are illegible at print size; show the exact prompts and coordinate string; name the AITW mirror; release anonymized code.
- State whether the five-seed extension of the headline cell was pre-specified (R2 W6), and label the 50+ uncorrected contrasts as descriptive.

**Reviewer points I discount**

- R1's comparison to ScreenSpot-v2 numbers (85–92% for 3B models): a different task with explicit referring expressions and no episode-level goal; not like-for-like. The low operating point and missing history are limitations to state, not evidence the setup is invalid.
- R1's "LiMAC is mischaracterized": the paper's sentence never says the VLM grounds; it is ambiguous, not wrong (R4 checked it and found it accurate). One clause fixes it.
- R2's inference that the five-seed extension followed a look at three-seed p-values, drawn from Table 8's n=1200 cell: Appendix A states that models in different tables sharing a seed are separately trained copies, so that cell says nothing about the headline's first three seeds, and the paper states the ordering was unchanged with them. Ask for pre-specification; do not treat as a flaw.
- R2's "effective n is roughly 30–40, not 250": that is the ρ=1 bound; the true design effect depends on the intra-episode correlation of the paired differences, which is likely well below 1. R2's own design-effect-of-2–3 calibration is the right one, and it leaves B's headline intact.
- R3's treatment of the unverified diagnosis as undermining contribution 4: three reviewers and I confirm the library does what the paper says; what is missing is a confirmation in the authors' own runs, a minor addition.
- R2's reading that C's −0.057 on taps-and-swipes is entirely a format failure: the mean-L2 arithmetic supports a parse-failure contribution, but explaining the full hit@0.10 deficit would require nearly every unparseable output to have been a hit. A partial explanation until the parsed-only table exists.
- R3's remark that the "grounded/faithful" framing is thin: the paper squarely fits the workshop's training-paradigm, failure-analysis and interpretability topics; not a decision factor.

**Decision**: Accept (poster), with the camera-ready revisions in items 1–4 below required.

The three reject leans are explicitly conditional on framing, and each of those reviewers writes that a reframed version would be a credible poster; the paper's body already contains every control that deflates its own headline (Tables 3 and 7, the end-to-end null, the retracted `inputs_embeds` result, the walked-back attention claim), so the problem is what the abstract, title and conclusion assert, not what the experiments show. Every consequential issue is fixable by rewriting or re-analysis of existing logs, and the two that benefit from new runs (a properly trained D-token and D-text on the controls) cost a few GPU-hours. What remains after the reframing is sound and useful to this audience: a matched-compute comparison of five ways to deliver a type signal with paired statistics at two levels, a clean-stream negative result, a reusable wrong/zero/class-mean intervention protocol for testing whether a conditioning signal is actually read, and a verified, silently failing library pitfall that anyone injecting soft prompts into Qwen2-VL will hit. That meets "sound, honest, useful" at a workshop that accepted small-scale and negative-result papers last year. It is not a spotlight: there is no crisp headline, since the positive result is conditional on a preprocessing artifact, the negative one is confounded by an untrained embedding, and several stars may not survive episode-level clustering.

**If the authors could make exactly five changes before the camera-ready or a resubmission, they should be (in priority order)**:

1. Rewrite the title, abstract, Section 5.1 heading and conclusion so the headline is the mechanism ranking plus the clean-stream null, the sentinel-class protection is presented as a diagnosis of a preprocessing choice, the end-to-end null and the wrong-type collapse are stated as the deployment findings, and every "returns to baseline / no mechanism beats it / does nothing" becomes an interval or an equivalence statement with a declared margin.
2. Recompute every interval and star with episodes as the bootstrap cluster, report episodes per validation slice, drop the boundary episode shared with training, and state which conclusions survive.
3. Report the displacement of the D-token rows from initialization and rerun D-token with a separate embedding learning rate or word-embedding initialization plus the frozen-slot control on the fixed path, then narrow or retract the refutation to match.
4. Carry D-text through Table 2 (from existing logs), Tables 3 and 7, a wrong-word intervention and the end-to-end test, and make the abstract's "6 to 10 points" match what those tables show.
5. Fix descriptions and presentation: state where the D-hook hook sits relative to the visual-feature merge and correct "every image token" if it is on the embedding module, tabulate C on parsed outputs with per-class parse rates, add hit@0.14, give per-class or fixed-slice scaling curves, fill or remove the empty appendices, make Figures 1 and 4 legible, and release anonymized code.
